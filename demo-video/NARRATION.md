# Meridiqa narration

Spoken by **en-NG-AbeoNeural** (Nigerian English, male), via [edge-tts](https://github.com/rany2/edge-tts) 7.x. No API key.
Rate −10%, pitch −3 Hz, so the delivery stays slow enough for a formal room.

Picture length with this read: 176.3 seconds.

Music is an original piano bed written for this film (66 bpm, D major, sparse chords). It is rendered with FluidSynth and the FluidR3 GM soundfont, which Frank Wen released under the MIT licence. Chorus is off. A little reverb is on. The bed ducks under the voice, swells at the open and the close, and the mix is loudness-normalised to −16 LUFS, true peak −1.5 dBTP.

The FluidR3 copyright notice is in `demo-video/audio/FLUID-R3-MIT.txt`.

## hook

The class was due at eight.

The hall is full.

The lecturer has not arrived.

## origin

A quality officer at Accra Technical University kept a WhatsApp group of class representatives.

They wrote: the lecturer is not in class.

We are still waiting.

Lateness fell, once lecturers knew someone was watching. But it lived on one phone, and leadership had nothing they could act on.

## problem

A whole university cannot run on one phone. The evidence is scattered. And no one can see who reported, or what was decided.

## meet

This is Meridiqa. Class representatives report each session. Meridiqa checks the timetable and the course outline, and raises what needs a decision.

## rep

The report is short, and it is filed from the phone.

The representative signs in under a stand-in name.

It follows the class already on the timetable.

## alert

If the lecturer is late, the representative sends an alert.

This class is seventy-one minutes late.

The lecturer can answer: I'm on my way. That answer is kept.

## flags

The rules raise the issue. People decide it. Absence, lateness, leaving early, and falling behind the outline are marked from the report.

## coverage

The topics taught are set against the course outline. Nine of twelve are covered, in line with this point in the semester.

## appeal

A head of department can appeal. Quality assurance decides.

One appeal is waiting. The reason is written down, and there are five days to review it.

## privacy

The real name stays sealed, until there is a reason.

That reason has to be written down.

Here, the name that came back was A.T.U. Class Rep 1.

The student list still shows only the stand-in name.

## roles

Quality assurance runs the day.

A head of department sees one department.

The Vice-Chancellor sees the whole university.

The technology team sets the rules.

## ask

The Vice-Chancellor can ask the week's record, in plain English.

Which lecturers have been late this week?

Six lecturers were each late once.

Abena Mensah.

Akua Mensah.

Ama Addo.

Adjoa Agyemang.

Efua Addo.

And Esi Agyemang.

## export

The same record can be taken into a meeting, as a spreadsheet. Date, course, presence, minutes late, and any marks.

## fairness

If two representatives disagree, that disagreement stays visible. In this sample, none are open.

## roadmap

The report comes first. A camera can come later, beside the class representative, as a second witness. That is the road ahead. It is not a feature today.

## close
